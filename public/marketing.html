How a case works

Every case runs the same loop you run in clinic, not the one a multiple-choice question tests. You never see the diagnosis until you've committed to your reasoning. Here's a real one — a runner's knee that isn't what it looks like.

Meet the patient

You get the presentation and the complaint — nothing labelled. Anterior knee pain, six weeks, worse on stairs, still running on it. Where do you start?

Commit to a differential

Before you touch the patient, rank what this could be. This list is what every test you choose next gets measured against — get the order wrong and the debrief calls it anchoring.

Test what discriminates

Pick the examinations that actually separate your top hypotheses — findings reveal as you go. Ordering everything "to be safe" is scored as over-investigation.

See the diagnosis — and your reasoning

Now the answer: tibial bone-stress injury, masquerading as patellofemoral pain. And a breakdown of every decision — 58% on this attempt, with the differential and disposition where it slipped — cited to the sources the case was built from.

It scores your reasoning, not your guess

A final-answer quiz can't tell anchoring from a lucky guess. Infera weights every decision in the encounter and names the error when your reasoning breaks down.

  • History (15%) Did you ask the discriminating question, or the obvious one?
  • Red-flag screening (25%) Recall-weighted — a missed screen is the expensive mistake.
  • Differential (20%) How close the true diagnosis landed to your top pick, and your ordering.
  • Test selection (15%) Indicated tests raise your score; non-indicated ones cost you.
  • Disposition (25%) The right level of care, on a five-rung escalation ladder.

The debrief names it directly: anchoring, premature closure, over-investigation, under- or over-escalation, incomplete examination. You leave knowing which reasoning habit to fix, not just which case you got wrong.
