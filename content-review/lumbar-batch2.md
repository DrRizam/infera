## 1. `lb2-r1` — Radiculopathy (recognition)

*Multiple choice* · Status: **UNVERIFIED**

**Stem:** A seated patient is asked to slump the thoracolumbar spine, then flex the neck, extend the knee, and dorsiflex the ankle, while the examiner watches for reproduction of leg symptoms and releases neck flexion to see whether symptoms ease. Which test is this?

**Correct answer:** Slump test

**Distractors:** Straight leg raise · Prone instability test · Modified Schober test

**Explanation (shown after answering):** This is the slump test — a seated neurodynamic sequence that progressively loads the neural structures (spinal flexion → cervical flexion → knee extension → ankle dorsiflexion). The structural differentiation step is what makes it a neurodynamic test rather than a hamstring stretch: if releasing neck flexion changes the leg symptoms, the symptoms are neural in origin.

**Pearl:** The release step is the test. Without structural differentiation, you've only measured hamstring length.

> 📚 **Citation:** Majlesi J, Togay H, Ünalan H, Toprak S. The sensitivity and specificity of the slump and the straight leg raising tests in patients with lumbar disc herniation. J Clin Rheumatol. 2008;14(2):87-91.

## 3. `lb2-r3` — Spinal stenosis (recognition)

*Discriminate two conditions* · Status: **UNVERIFIED**

**Stem:** A 71-year-old reports bilateral leg heaviness and aching when walking. Which reported feature points toward NEUROGENIC rather than vascular claudication?

**Correct answer:** Symptoms ease with sitting or leaning forward on a shopping trolley, and cycling is tolerated well

**Distractors:** Symptoms ease within 2–3 minutes of simply standing still · Symptoms are reproduced at a consistent, predictable walking distance every time · The legs are cool with diminished pedal pulses

**Explanation (shown after answering):** Neurogenic claudication is posture-dependent: lumbar flexion opens the canal, so leaning on a trolley, sitting, or cycling relieves symptoms, while walking upright and extension provoke them. Vascular claudication is demand-dependent: it comes on at a reproducible distance, eases with rest in any position within a few minutes, and pairs with vascular signs. Both can coexist in this age group — check pulses regardless.

**Pearl:** Ask 'can you cycle?' — flexion-tolerant walking limitation is the cleanest bedside discriminator.

## 5. `lb2-p1` — Radiculopathy (procedure)

*Order the steps / rank* · Status: **UNVERIFIED**

**Stem:** Order the steps for performing a straight leg raise.

**Correct order:**
1. Position the patient supine with the head, trunk, and pelvis neutral, and explain what you will do
2. Passively raise the symptomatic leg with the knee extended and the hip in neutral rotation
3. Stop at the point the patient's familiar leg symptoms are reproduced and note the angle
4. Lower slightly below the symptomatic angle, then add ankle dorsiflexion or cervical flexion to differentiate neural from musculotendinous origin

**Explanation (shown after answering):** Standardise the position first: hip rotation, pelvic tilt, and trunk position all change the angle at which symptoms appear. Raise until FAMILIAR symptoms appear — posterior thigh tightness is not a positive. The sensitising manoeuvre at the end is what separates neural mechanosensitivity from hamstring tension.

> 📚 **Citation:** van der Windt DA, et al. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2010;(2):CD007431 (test description and accuracy).

## 6. `lb2-p2` — Radiculopathy (procedure)

*Order the steps / rank* · Status: **UNVERIFIED**

**Stem:** Order the components of the slump test.

**Correct order:**
1. Seat the patient upright at the edge of the plinth with the hands behind the back
2. Ask the patient to slump the thoracic and lumbar spine while you maintain the sacrum vertical
3. Add cervical flexion and hold it
4. Actively or passively extend the knee, then add ankle dorsiflexion

**Explanation (shown after answering):** Load the neural system from the top down, then differentiate. Holding the sacrum vertical stops the patient escaping into posterior pelvic tilt. Releasing neck flexion at the end is the differentiation step — leg symptoms that ease when the neck is released implicate neural tissue rather than hamstring.

## 7. `lb2-p3` — Low back mechanics (procedure)

*Order the steps / rank* · Status: **UNVERIFIED**

**Stem:** Order the steps for the modified Schober measurement of lumbar flexion.

**Correct order:**
1. With the patient standing, mark the midline at the level of the posterior superior iliac spines
2. Mark a second point 10 cm above and a third 5 cm below that landmark
3. Ask the patient to bend forward as far as comfortable, keeping the knees straight
4. Measure the new distance between the upper and lower marks
5. Subtract the original 15 cm to report the increase in lumbar flexion

**Explanation (shown after answering):** The modified Schober uses a 15 cm span (10 above, 5 below the PSIS line) and reports the INCREASE on flexion, not the absolute distance. It is a segmental measure of lumbar mobility that deliberately excludes hip flexion — which is precisely why fingertip-to-floor distance is not interchangeable with it.

**Pearl:** Fingertip-to-floor measures the whole kinetic chain. Schober measures the lumbar spine. Don't swap them between visits.

> 📚 **Citation:** Magee DJ. Orthopedic Physical Assessment. 6th ed. Elsevier Saunders; 2014 (modified Schober procedure).

## 8. `lb2-p4` — Radiculopathy (procedure)

*Order the steps / rank* · Status: **UNVERIFIED**

**Correct order:**
